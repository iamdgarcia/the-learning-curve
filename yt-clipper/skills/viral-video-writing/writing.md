# Writing lines people say out loud

## How it should sound
- **Pace:** 3.6-4.6 spoken words a second. A 40 second video is about 160 words.
- **Sentences:** 8-16 words, joined with "and", "so", "because". Flowing talk, not stacked fragments.
- **Numbers:** written the way they are said in the voiceover ("a hundred and fifty K a month"), shown as digits on screen.
- **Reading level:** aim for grade 4-6. Short words win.
- **Openers that work:** a result or an experiment ("I gave Claude…"), a question ("What happens when you ask…"), or news ("Claude just killed…").

## Anti-AI writing rules (each AI tell makes people swipe)
People can hear AI writing in one line. These are the patterns that give it away, and what to say instead.

**Sentence shapes**
- **Metaphor punchlines:** "a panic button with a subscription attached", "a goldmine", "the secret sauce". Say the plain fact instead.
- **Dramatic one-word sentences:** "Panic." "Simple." "Wild." One per script at most.
- **"It's not X, it's Y" and "not just X but Y".** Just say Y.
- **Rule-of-three lists inside one sentence:** "faster, smarter, and cheaper". Pick the one that matters.
- **Rhetorical questions in the body:** "The best part?" "Why does this matter?" Only the hook can be a question.
- **Fake suspense:** "Spoiler:", "Plot twist:", "But here's where it gets interesting." Show the twist instead of announcing it.

**Filler and stage directions**
- "Here's the thing." "Let that sink in." "Read the fine print." "The truth is." "In today's world." "Buckle up." "Imagine this."
- "Whether you're a beginner or a pro…" "It's worth noting that…" "At the end of the day…"
- Hedging: "arguably", "potentially", "in many ways". Say it or cut it.

**Words that sound like AI**
delve, unlock, unleash, harness, leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionary, transformative,
landscape, realm, tapestry, journey (as a metaphor), elevate, empower, supercharge, deep dive, next level, dive in.

**Punctuation**
- Em dashes and semicolons. Nobody speaks them. Use a period or "and".
- Emoji in the script itself. Save them for the caption, if at all.

**Claims**
- "I tried this and…" when you didn't, invented results, invented quotes. The comments will catch it.

**The fix for all of it:** read the line out loud. If you wouldn't say it to a friend across a table, rewrite it.

## Technical, but anyone understands it
The viewer is a smart friend who doesn't work in your field. An expert should nod, a beginner should follow every line.
1. **Outcome first, mechanism second.** "Claude answers questions about your data" before "it runs SQL on your tables".
2. **Explain every technical term the first time,** in 10 words or fewer: "MCP servers. Plug-ins that let Claude use your other apps."
3. **No stacked nouns.** "Scoped memory, keychain view, durable sandbox" means nothing. Pick the 2 that matter and say what they do.
4. **Result verbs, not plumbing verbs.** "fetches config" → "checks your settings". "triages your inbox" → "sorts your inbox".
5. **One comparison per item, max,** and it must explain, not decorate. OK: "you're using Claude like Google". Not OK: "a Ferrari you drive at 10 km/h".
6. **Commands go on screen, the voice says what they do.** Voice: "one line and it's installed." Screen: the exact command.
7. **Give a number meaning once:** "over 57,000 GitHub stars, that's GitHub's version of likes".

## Before → after
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "These are the five MCP servers you need right now." | "These are the five MCP servers you need right now. MCP servers are plug-ins that let Claude use your other apps." |
| "No outdated code examples, no made-up APIs." | "No outdated code, no made-up commands that don't exist." |
| "It runs on its own virtual machine." | "It runs on its own computer in the cloud, so your laptop can be off." |
| "Third, CRO." | "Third, CRO. Getting more visitors to actually sign up." |

## The CTA
- Shape: "Follow me and comment KEYWORD and I'll send you [the real thing]."
- No payload to send? Use comment bait that starts a conversation: "Comment the next app you want me to break down."
- One keyword, one word, not inside common words.
