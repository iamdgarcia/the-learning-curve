# Story: how to build a video people watch to the end

A list of tips gets saved. A story gets watched to the end, shared and rewatched.
Even a 30 second tutorial is stronger when it has a before, a problem and an after.

## The arc in one line
**Where you started → what went wrong → the moment it turned → where you are now → proof.**

Every beat has to raise the stakes or pay something off. If a beat does neither, cut it.

## The 7 story beats (for a personal story or a case study)

| # | Beat | Job | Example (from a reel at 103K views) |
|---|------|-----|------|
| 1 | **The decision** | Open on the most dramatic choice, not the backstory. | "I'm dropping out of high school." at 0:01 |
| 2 | **The villain** | Someone or something says it will fail. Now the viewer wants to see them proven wrong. | "You're gonna be a nobody." at 0:06 |
| 3 | **The promise** | The hero answers back. This is the open loop the whole video closes. | "Not yet. But I will." |
| 4 | **The grind** | Short, fast scenes of trying and failing. Show, don't explain. | cold calls, "not interested", delivering pizza in the rain |
| 5 | **Rock bottom** | The lowest point, said out loud, plus the person who stays. | "I can't keep going." / "We'll do whatever it takes." at 0:41 |
| 6 | **The turn** | One small win that changes everything. Keep it simple. | "Yes, let's do it." (the first sale) at 0:58 |
| 7 | **The payoff** | Go back to the people from beat 1 and 2. The callback is the reward. | pulling up at the school, then "Yes Mom, and I got you one too" at 2:05 |

Then **proof**: the last 23 seconds of that reel are real footage. The story makes people feel it, and the real clips make them believe it.

## Rules that make a story work
- **Open in the middle of the action.** No "so a few years ago". The first line is a decision, a conflict or a result.
- **Give the viewer someone to root against** in the first 10 seconds. A doubter, a rule, a number.
- **Time jumps are re-hooks.** "3 months later" and "years later" title cards restart attention and skip the boring parts.
- **One emotion per scene.** Fear, doubt, grind, relief, pride. Don't mix them in one shot.
- **Callbacks are the payoff.** The ending should answer the first 10 seconds: the same people, the same place, the opposite result.
- **Hold the big music back.** In the 103K reel the song waits two minutes and fades in 6 seconds before the real footage. When the music finally drops, it feels earned.
- **Real people, real facts.** Never invent a struggle, a number or a quote. If it didn't happen, it doesn't go in.
- **End on the last word.** When the story is told, the video ends. No outro.

## Story shapes that keep working
- **Hero's journey:** start → struggle → turn → transformation → new reality. Best for personal stories.
- **Before / after:** show the after first, then rewind. "This is my mom's new car. Two years ago I was cold calling from her kitchen."
- **The almost-quit:** struggle → almost quit → one thing changes → callback payoff.
- **The bet:** "Everyone said X wouldn't work, so I tried it for 30 days." Each day is a re-hook.
- **The mystery:** open with a strange result, then reveal how it happened one clue at a time (the "I reverse engineered…" format).

## Story inside a short tutorial
Even a 40 second "how to" gets better with a 3-beat story wrapped around it:
1. **The problem, as a moment:** "I posted every day for a month and nothing went over 1,000 views."
2. **The fix, as steps:** the actual value.
3. **The after, as proof:** "the next video did 100K." Only if it's true.

## Checklist
- [ ] Does the first line show a decision, conflict or result?
- [ ] Is there a doubter or a stake in the first 10 seconds?
- [ ] Is there a clear lowest point?
- [ ] Does the ending call back to the opening?
- [ ] Is every fact real?
